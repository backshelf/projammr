Conversations about the music happen in comments, and the activity feed keeps the record of who changed what.
Both follow the project's roles.

## Two places to comment on a track

A track has two kinds of comments, and they stay separate:

- **Comments on the track** are about the song as a whole: its direction, its arrangement, what it still needs.
  They sit in the track page's **Comments** section.
- **[Comments on an audio version](/docs/guide/waveform-and-comments/#timecoded-comments)** are about one bounce, and each can be pinned to a moment in it, which is
  where mix notes belong. Each version keeps its own, under **Comments on v1**, **Comments on v2** and so on.

Projects, collections and sessions have a **Comments** section of their own too, for notes about them.

## Who can comment

Every member can post comments, viewers included. You can edit or delete your own comments, and nobody else's.

Visitors on a [share page](/docs/collaboration/share-links/) can comment on the audio without an account.
Their comments land on the version they were listening to, next to the members' comments on it.

When someone deletes their account, the comments they left in projects other people are still in stay, shown as
from "Deleted user".

## The activity feed

The **Activity** section records what happens in a project, newest first. It is folded away until you open it.

- On the **project** page it covers the whole project.
- On a **collection** or **track** page it covers just that collection or track.

Each entry says who did what, and when: created, updated or deleted something, or commented. An update lists the
fields that changed, with their old and new values; long text such as notes and descriptions is left out. A comment on an audio version shows on its track's feed with
the moment it was made at, such as "commented on" the track "at 1:24".

Every member can see the activity feed, whatever their role. Visitors on a share page never see it.

Sessions keep a history of their own: their saves, recorded by dit. [The dit page](/dit/) covers where those come
from.
