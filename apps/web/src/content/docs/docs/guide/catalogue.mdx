Projammr organises your music in four layers: **projects** hold **collections** and **tracks**, and tracks have
**sessions** behind them. This page explains what each one is and how they fit together, so you can decide where
things go.

## Projects

A project is an artist or a band: the home for its collections, tracks and sessions. It has a name, a description
and links to anywhere the project lives online. It is also the unit you share with people: members join a
project, and their role in it decides what they can change.

Everything else lives inside exactly one project.

## Collections

A collection is anything a set of tracks ships as: an album, an EP, a single, or a pile of loose ends you want to
keep together. A collection has a title, notes, a status, a release date and a UPC. Projammr does not ask whether
it is an album or an EP; call it what you like.

A collection keeps its tracks in a **running order**. The number is part of the collection, not the track, so the
same song can be track 2 on the EP and track 7 on the album.

## Tracks

A track is a song, with its own sessions, audio and notes. It carries a title, its artists, a status, a priority,
an explicit flag, its ISRC and ISWC, notes, labels, and its lyrics.

- **A track can be on several collections at once**, like a single that also appears on the album.
- **A track does not need a collection.** Make one straight in the project for a song that is not heading
  anywhere yet.

A track's audio lives in its **versions**: every bounce you upload is one, and one is the primary. See
[Versions and uploads](/docs/guide/audio-versions/).

## Sessions

A session is the DAW project file behind a track: an Ableton set, for instance. When [dit](/dit/) reads a set on
your computer, its session in Projammr carries what the set says about itself: tempo, key, time signature, the
tracks and devices inside it, its samples, and a history of every save. See [Sessions](/docs/guide/sessions/).

- **A session does not need a track.** dit can record a set before you have decided which song it belongs to, and you
  can assign it later.
- **One session per track can be its main session**: the one that carries the track's work. A track shows the
  key and BPM its main session reports.

You can also add a session by hand, with just a name, notes and the DAW it lives in. A session made that way has
no file behind it, so it never shows tempo, structure or history.

## How the pages are laid out

Every project, collection, track and session has its own page, and all four are built the same way.

/>

- **The masthead** across the top shows where you are (the crumbs back up to the project), what kind of page it
  is, the title, and a line of the key facts. It is lit by the page's [artwork](/docs/guide/artwork/). On a
  track, the masthead also holds the player and the version picker, and on a collection a play button for the
  whole running order.
- **The body** runs down the page in sections: notes and artwork first, then the page's own main table or
  content, then relations, attachments, comments and activity.
- **The rail** on the right holds the details: status, progress, dates, and the properties you can change in
  place. On a narrow window it moves between the masthead and the body.

When you open a track from a collection, the masthead reads "Track 3 of 9" or similar, with arrows to step to the
previous and next track in that collection's running order.

### What each page shows

| Page       | Main sections                                                                                        | In the rail                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Project    | Description, artwork, collections, plugins, the work heatmap, links, attachments, comments, activity | Collection count, links, members, created and updated dates                                                                                           |
| Collection | Notes, artwork, tracks in running order, attachments, comments, activity                             | Status and progress, loudness, spectrum, tempo and keys across the tracks, release date, UPC, sharing, runtime                                        |
| Track      | Notes, artwork, lyrics, sessions, relations, attachments, comments, activity                         | Key and BPM, loudness and spectrum, the "Worked on" heatmap, sharing, ISRC and ISWC, status, priority, explicit, collections, labels                  |
| Session    | Notes, structure, attachments, samples, history, comments                                            | Repository, local file path and dates, the main-session star, machine, and what the DAW reported: tempo, time signature, key, tracks, scenes, version |

## Who can change what

[Your role in a project](/docs/collaboration/members-and-roles/#the-roles) decides what you can do in it. Viewers can open and play everything. Editors, admins and
owners can also create, change and delete collections, tracks and sessions, upload artwork and files, and manage
statuses and labels. Admins manage members; only the owner can delete the project.
