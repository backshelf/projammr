Projammr measures every version you add, so you can check a mix without opening a meter plugin: how loud it is,
where it peaks, how its loudness moves through the song, and how its tone compares with the other versions and the
rest of the record.

## Loudness and true peak

Beside the version menu, each version shows its integrated loudness and true peak, such as
"−16.3 LUFS · TP −1.2". Hover a unit (or tap it on a phone) for what it means:

- **LUFS** is the integrated loudness across the whole track. Streaming services play music at about −14 LUFS.
- **TP**, the true peak, is the highest level between samples. Keeping it below −1 dB avoids clipping after
  encoding.

While a version is still being measured, it reads "measuring…".

## The strips under the waveform

Three strips can run under a version's waveform, lined up with it second by second. Turn each on or off with its
button; your browser remembers which you like.

- **Loudness**: how loud the song is from moment to moment (its short-term loudness), on a scale from −28 to
  0 LUFS, with a dotted line at the −14 streaming level.
- **Spectrogram**: where the energy sits across the frequency range over time, tinted in the track's colour.
- **Change**: where this version differs from the one before it. The stronger the bar, the more changed in that
  second. A caption sums it up, such as "38% changed · most in 1:32–2:10"; select it to jump to the part that
  changed most.

## The track's rail

- **Loudness** shows the version's reading, and a live meter while the track plays.
- **Spectrum** draws the average spectrum of every measured version of the track in one window, with the version
  you are on picked out. It is the quickest way to see a mix get brighter or lose its low end from one bounce to
  the next.
- **Key** and **BPM**, from the main session or detected in the audio. See
  [Versions and uploads](/docs/guide/audio-versions/#key-and-bpm).

### The live meter

While a track plays, the loudness meter moves with it: a bar for the **momentary** loudness (the last 0.4
seconds), a needle for the **short-term** loudness (the last 3 seconds), and the **peak**, each with its figure as
**M**, **S** and **Peak**. It shows on the track's rail, the collection's rail and in the
[now-playing sheet](/docs/guide/player-and-queue/#now-playing).

## Across a collection

A collection's rail sums up its tracks, so you can see whether a record hangs together.

<Screenshot

  alt="A collection's rail: the loudness of each track on one scale, the collection's spectrum, its tempo range and the keys on a circle of fifths"
/>

- **Loudness** gives the median loudness of the tracks, with every track marked on one scale and the range they
  span.
- **Spectrum** draws the average spectrum across the tracks, inside the band between the quietest and loudest.
- **Tempo** gives the median BPM, the range, and each track on a scale from 60 to 200 BPM.
- **Keys** puts each track on a circle of fifths: "Major outside, minor inside". While one of the collection's
  tracks plays, its dot pulses in time.

Each of these counts only the tracks that have been measured, and says so until they all are. While a track in
the collection plays, its live level shows over the summary.

<Aside>
  The readings use each track's primary version. Choose the primary with the star in the track's version menu;
  see [Versions and uploads](/docs/guide/audio-versions/#choosing-the-version-you-hear).
</Aside>
