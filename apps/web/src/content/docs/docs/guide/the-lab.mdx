The Lab is a set of listening tools that sit between the player and your speakers. Slow a part down to learn it,
check the mix in mono, hear it roughly the way it would sound on a phone or in a car, or try a little EQ before you
go back to the session. Nothing in the Lab changes the file: it shapes only what you hear, in your browser, and
your settings stay in that browser for next time.

## Opening the Lab

Play something, then:

- press <kbd>L</kbd>;
- choose **Open Lab** from the player's **More controls**; or
- open the [now-playing sheet](/docs/guide/player-and-queue/#now-playing) and switch to its Lab pane.

The [command palette](/docs/guide/search-and-shortcuts/) has **Open the Lab** while something is playing.

## Time and pitch

**Time & pitch** changes speed and pitch in four ways:

- **Speed only**: faster or slower at the same pitch.
- **Pitch only**: higher or lower at the same speed.
- **Linked**: speed and pitch move together, like a record played faster or slower.
- **Independent**: set each on its own.

Speed goes from half speed to double, and pitch from 12 semitones down to 12 up. **Reset** puts both back.

## The rack

Three effects, each with its own on and off switch:

- **EQ**: eight bands, drawn as a curve over the live spectrum. Drag the curve to shape it. **Bands** lists the
  active bands with their filter type, frequency, gain and Q, and **Add band** adds another.
- **Comp**: a compressor with **Threshold**, **Ratio**, **Attack**, **Release** and **Makeup**, and a meter showing
  how much it is turning the music down.
- **Verb**: a reverb with four rooms (**Booth**, **Room**, **Hall** and **Club**), how much of it you hear
  (**Wet**) and how long before it starts (**Pre-delay**).

## Hear it as

Auditions let you check a mix the way an engineer would, without touching it.

| Row     | Choices                                                                                                           |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Stereo  | **Mono**, **Mid**, **Side**, **L**, **R**, and **Ø R**, which flips the right channel's polarity                  |
| Monitor | **Crossfeed**; **Dim −20**, which turns everything down 20 dB; and **−14 LUFS**, which plays the version at the streaming level |
| Bands   | **Low**, **Low-mid**, **Mid**, **High-mid** and **Air**: solo one or more parts of the frequency range             |
| Device  | **Phone**, **Laptop**, **Earbuds**, **Car**, **Club** and **Radio**: an impression of hearing it on that kind of speaker |

**−14 LUFS** needs the version to have been measured first.

Two auditions have their own keys: press <kbd>H</kbd> then <kbd>M</kbd> for mono, and <kbd>H</kbd> then
<kbd>D</kbd> for dim. In the command palette, `lab` and a space toggles any audition by name.

### Match EQ

**Match EQ to** picks another version of the same track, and shapes the one you are hearing toward that version's
tone. Use it to hear whether a new mix has drifted from one you liked. It needs at least one other version that
has been measured.

## Bounce

**Bounce** records exactly what the Lab is playing, with every setting, and attaches it to the track as a 24-bit
FLAC file. The version's waveform fills in as it renders, the caption counts through "Rendering" and "Uploading"
to "Bounced", and the finished file is linked by name.

Anyone in the project can use the Lab. Bouncing adds a file to the track, so it needs an editor, admin or owner.

<Aside type="caution" title="On a phone">
  While the Lab is shaping the sound, playback stops when the screen locks. The now-playing sheet warns you:
  "Lab on · playback stops when the screen locks".
</Aside>
